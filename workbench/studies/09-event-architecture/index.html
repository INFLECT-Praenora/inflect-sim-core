Claim

The discrete sepsis events can be lifted out of the SBML into the vivarium layer and the hybrid still reproduces the full model — at no runtime cost versus the fastest exact option. The event-free SBML core, driven by viva-impulse's ScheduledEvent (E_infect) and ThresholdSwitch (E_tlrOn/Off) through the TelluriumImpulseBridge, reproduces the full event-driven model (same proto_valve continuous core, events relocated): over a 2 h severe run the hemodynamics (MAP, SBP, HR, SVR) agree to ~1e-4 max relative and tissue integrity to ~3e-4. Blood pathogen tracks with a brief ~20% transient during the steep infection rise that converges to ~0.2% — the one visible residual, and it is purely the tick-quantization of the infection start (the ScheduledEvent fires at the first 10 s tick at or after infectionStart, versus the SBML event's exact root-find; a smaller interval shrinks it). Runtime (same roadrunner engine + Composite harness, this machine, 2 h severity 3): full sepsis_sbml 284 s, proto_valve 236 s, hybrid 233 s — the hybrid matches the fastest exact option and is ~1.22x faster than the full event-driven model, because its continuous core is event-free (no CVODE root returns) and the lifted events are cheap. So against the other run options the event-driven format is prediction-faithful AND runtime-competitive, and it buys explicit, swappable events (a different protocol, an added controller, retimed dosing) with no SBML surgery — unlike the learned surrogate (study 04), which is ~1000x faster but inadmissible on 8/9 severe observables. This holds for the SLOW, timescale-separated events (infection onset, the TLR mode switch); the fast per-beat event stays in the engine (study 08) — and the extraction report confirms E_beat is BioGears' own per-cardiac-cycle measurement event, intrinsic to the beat and not separable to the tick.

Background

Question BioGears is event-driven, and the SBML extraction encodes that as SBML events run by roadrunner's root-finder — opaque to the composition engine. Can we use vivarium's own event-driven capabilities instead: lift the model's discrete events OUT of the SBML and express them as first-class process-bigraph constructs, and still reproduce the model?

Model & approach

Take the event-free SBML core (proto_valve with ALL events removed: the per-beat E_beat and pulOpen, and now the infection E_infect and the TLR hysteresis E_tlrOn/E_tlrOff) and supply the infection and TLR events from the vivarium layer as general viva-impulse Steps — a ScheduledEvent (timed infection) and a ThresholdSwitch (TLR hysteresis) — coupled to the engine through a parameters_in bridge. Measure how closely the hybrid reproduces the full event-driven model, and state the cost.

Result

Lifting an event to the composition layer is correct — and useful — exactly when the event's timescale SEPARATES from the continuous core. The slow, rarely-firing infection and TLR events lift cleanly and reproduce the model; the fast per-beat event does not, and stays in the engine.

◆ Lifting an event to the composition layer is correct — and useful — exactly when the event's timescale SEPARATES from the continuous core. The slow, rarely-firing infection and TLR events lift cleanly and reproduce the model; the fast per-beat event does not, and stays in the engine.
lifting-the-slow-events-to-vivarium-reproduces-the-model other supported literature
Why: Vivarium's event-driven machinery (trigger-fired Steps, multi-rate scheduling) coordinates discrete transitions with a continuous core at the tick boundary. That is the right tool for events whose timescale is well separated from the fast dynamics: the infection fires once and the TLR mode flips a handful of times over a 48 h course, so quantizing them to a 10 s tick is negligible — and the payoff is real, since the events become explicit, composable objects (swap the infection protocol, chain the antibiotic controller from study 06, retime a dose) with no SBML surgery. The per-beat event is the opposite case: it fires ~205k times and is tightly coupled to the stiff cardiovascular ODE, so splitting it across the tick-quantized composition layer would cost far more than it buys — there the right move is reduction inside the engine (study 08), not relocation. The extraction report (Sauro) confirms this split at the source: E_beat is BioGears' own per-cardiac-cycle measurement (pressures captured per beat, read by the baroreflex), intrinsic to the beat; the infection and AIR logic are the slow discrete layer. The honest placement: this study demonstrates the slow-event half of the design rule — lift the separated events, keep (or reduce) the coupled ones — with the SAME general viva-impulse constructs that run the standalone bouncing-ball and thermostat demos, now driving a stiff physiological engine.
