Performance — where the compute goes, and the surrogate speedup

Q How fast is the simulation core, and where does the compute go? For one matched 48 h sepsis run, what does each engine cost, what dominates that cost, and how much does the learned surrogate save?

Claim

For one matched 48 h severe-sepsis run the same spec spans 4+ orders of magnitude of compute: BioGears (C++ physiology) ~3.38 h, Tellurium (exact SBML via roadrunner/ CVODE) ~2.81 h, and the PyTorch surrogate ~0.2 s (~40,000x faster than the exact SBML path). The exact engines spend essentially all of that time integrating the stiff 91-state ODE — a stiff implicit solver (CVODE) is required, so a faster or Rust explicit integrator cannot speed it up; the real levers are a better stiff solver, GPU-parallel patients, or a surrogate. The surrogate replaces the ODE solve with one forward pass: a live per-step micro-benchmark on this machine measures ~526 ms for one exact CVODE step of the real model vs ~0.04 ms for one surrogate forward pass — the exact per-step cost is ~13,000x the surrogate's. The surrogate is a fast APPROXIMATE tier (bounded, not yet clinically accurate), not a drop-in replacement.

Background

Question How fast is the simulation core, and where does the compute go? For one matched 48 h sepsis run, what does each engine cost, what dominates that cost, and how much does the learned surrogate save?

Model & approach

Profile the compute across engines on one matched run: the full-run wall-clock (BioGears, Tellurium), the dominant cost (stiff ODE integration), whether a faster integrator can help, and the surrogate's per-step speedup — with a live, reproducible micro-benchmark behind the full-run numbers.
