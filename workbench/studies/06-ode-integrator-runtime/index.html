ODE integrator runtime: can a Rust/compiled solver speed up the SBML model?

Q The SBML extraction is attractive partly as a potentially faster route than the full BioGears C++ engine. Would reimplementing its ODE *integrator* — for example in Rust, the way our viva-hybrid engine does with a fixed-step RK4 — make the SBML sepsis model meaningfully faster than the libroadrunner (CVODE) path we already use? This study separates the two different "engines" people mean: study 04 swapped the *composition* engine (Python process-bigraph vs Rust viva-core); this study is about the *numerical ODE integrator* that actually advances the 91 states.

Claim

The sepsis ODE is stiff, and that settles the question. An explicit fixed-step RK4 — viva-hybrid's integrator — is numerically unstable on the real model: it diverges to non-finite values for every step dt >= 0.05 s. At steps fine enough to stay finite, it converges to the *wrong* trajectory: even at dt = 0.001 s (172.8 million steps for a single 48 h simulation) the fast cardiovascular states are wrong by more than 100x (vena-cava pressure 406 vs the true 1.58; aortic pressure 1000 vs 38; 30 of 91 states off by >1%, 5 by >100%), while the slow inflammation and metabolic states track (median error 0.3%). The adaptive explicit method (rk45) is stability-limited to steps so small it could not finish a 60 s window in 45 s. The one solver that works — a stiff BDF method (CVODE) — is already compiled C (SUNDIALS) inside libroadrunner and integrates the window in seconds. The stiffness lives in the cardiovascular loop, which is exactly the subsystem where the engine and the extraction diverge (studies 03, 05). An independent trial (the viva-hybrid repo) shows that even on a *non-stiff* model, where explicit integration is valid, a from-scratch Rust engine beat compiled numba by only ~2.0x. So reimplementing the integrator is not an order-of-magnitude speed lever for this model. The real ODE-speed levers are a better or warm-started stiff solver, GPU parallelism across many virtual patients, or a reduced-order surrogate.

Background

Question The SBML extraction is attractive partly as a potentially faster route than the full BioGears C++ engine. Would reimplementing its ODE *integrator* — for example in Rust, the way our viva-hybrid engine does with a fixed-step RK4 — make the SBML sepsis model meaningfully faster than the libroadrunner (CVODE) path we already use? This study separates the two different "engines" people mean: study 04 swapped the *composition* engine (Python process-bigraph vs Rust viva-core); this study is about the *numerical ODE integrator* that actually advances the 91 states.
Hypothesis No. The BioGears sepsis core is a stiff ODE system, so an explicit fixed-step integrator (viva-hybrid's RK4) is the wrong tool — it will be unstable at any practical step size. And the correct tool, a stiff implicit solver (CVODE/BDF), is already compiled C inside libroadrunner, so a reimplementation in another language cannot buy an order of magnitude. The speedup people hope for is not in the integrator.

Model & approach

On the real 91-state model, compare roadrunner's integrators on a representative window: cvode (stiff BDF, compiled C — the default), rk45 (adaptive explicit), and rk4 (fixed-step explicit — the same algorithm as the viva-hybrid Rust engine). Sweep the RK4 step size; measure stability and accuracy against a tight stiff reference; extrapolate the step count to a 48 h run; and identify which state variables an explicit method fails on. Cross-reference the independent viva-hybrid trial, which measured a from-scratch Rust integrator against compiled numba on a non-stiff model.
